Planning your kitchen

The heart of the home deserves a careful plan

A kitchen has to handle early-morning routines, weeknight dinners, homework, guests and everything in between.

Our approach is to understand those routines first, then design cabinetry, surfaces, lighting and layout to support them. The result should feel natural to use and pleasant to spend time in.

Cabinetry & storage

Storage that makes sense for your routine

Cabinets set the tone of a kitchen and carry most of its daily workload. We look at what you store, how often you reach for it and where it should live.

  • Deep drawers and pull-outsEasier access than reaching into the back of a base cabinet.
  • Dedicated zonesKeep pots, baking supplies and everyday dishes near where they are used.
  • Hardware and door stylesChosen for comfort in use as well as appearance.

Countertops & surfaces

Surfaces that look good and live well

Countertops and backsplashes take the most wear and make the biggest visual statement. We help you compare materials in terms of appearance, care and how you actually use the kitchen.

  • Compare real samplesView them in your own lighting and next to your cabinet color.
  • Think about upkeepSealing, stain resistance and heat tolerance vary between materials.
  • Plan the edges and seamsSmall details influence both comfort and appearance.

Islands, lighting and finishes

Islands & layouts

An island can add prep space and seating, but only if there is room to move around it comfortably.

  • Clearances for walking and opening doors
  • Work zones for cooking and cleanup
  • Seating that fits your household

Lighting & fixtures

Layered lighting makes a kitchen safer to work in and more pleasant in the evening.

  • Task lighting over work areas
  • Pendants and ambient light
  • Faucets and sinks suited to your habits

Finishes & materials

Color, texture and metal finishes should feel consistent across the whole room.

  • A limited, coordinated palette
  • Hardware and fixture finishes that agree
  • Flooring and wall colors that support the plan

Frequently asked questions

Kitchen remodeling FAQs

Start with how you use the kitchen now: what works, what frustrates you and who uses the space. Think about priorities (storage, seating, lighting, appliances), how long you can be without a working kitchen and whether you plan to change the layout or keep it. Having clear goals makes every later decision simpler.

Often the biggest gains come from small changes: deep drawers instead of lower shelves, a tall pantry cabinet, better corner solutions and keeping the sink, stove and refrigerator easy to reach. Clear walkways and enough counter space beside the cooktop and sink also make a kitchen feel larger and more usable.

Begin with how hard each surface will work, then consider appearance. Look at physical samples in your own lighting, compare care requirements and keep the palette coordinated so cabinetry, counters, backsplash and hardware feel like one design.

Helpful details include the approximate size of the kitchen, whether the layout will change, the type of cabinetry and surfaces you prefer, any appliances you plan to replace, photos of the current space and your preferred timing. A rough budget range also helps shape realistic options. Final pricing and schedules are confirmed only after a full review.
